Layer 1 · Overview and findings

Does a scheduling harness still earn its keep against current models?

A pre-registered ablation. Three Claude models (Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 5.5) worked the same 38 multi-turn scheduling scenarios three ways: bare, with raw calendar and mail tools and the rules in plain words; inside Winnie's harness with its guards computing but not enforcing; and inside the full harness. 5,040 graded runs over 32 scenarios the harness was built against and 6 written after its last change. Code graders frozen before the matrix; a model judge for prose, calibrated against 30 hand labels. Run 2026-11-09 to 14.

Why this study exists

Winnie is Bob Baird's executive-assistant agent. It reads his mail, holds his calendar and schedules meetings with other people under rules he has stated: his hours, the notice he wants, the buffer between calls, which decisions are his. He built its scheduling harness because, in his experience, models did not reliably stay inside prompt boundaries on temporal tasks once a thread ran to several turns. The harness gives the model a rendered plan state at every step, tools that cannot misreport what they did, and guards that refuse an action outside the rules.

Two questions follow. Does the harness still improve on a bare model as models improve, and which of its parts does the work? And what does the harness itself get wrong? The second matters because a bench that runs thousands of scenarios through a harness finds the bugs one user does not, and because a study that only flattered the harness would be worth less than one that found its faults.

How it was built. Bob wrote the research questions, the four hypotheses and every expected outcome before the first run, ruled on each edge case as a dated, numbered ruling, hand-labelled the texts that calibrate the prose judge, and read the runs. Claude sessions under his direction built the bench, the graders and the scenarios, ran the matrix and drafted this page and the write-up. Every commit in the repository carries both names.

The verdict

Yes, on the problems it was built for. With the harness, the models reached the right outcome in about 9 of 10 runs; alone, about 7 of 10. The lift is 20 to 25 points on every model.

Most of the help is the scaffolding, not the guards. Giving the model a clean picture of the plan and tools that cannot lie does nearly all the work. The guards that refuse bad actions add 2 to 6 points on top.

On brand-new problems, the model alone did about as well. On six scenarios written after the harness's last change, the harness's edge was +4, +3 and −7 of 80. The one failure that showed up there was the harness's own.

What this means for Winnie

  • Keep the scaffolding. The rendered plan state and the honest tools are where the value is, and they help every model, including the cheapest.
  • Treat the guards as a backstop, and keep them. They buy 2 to 6 points, and the runs they catch are the ones that matter most: a yes on the wrong time, a slot that has already passed, a wrong time zone, a booking Bob should have been asked about.
  • Fix the stale yes. After Bob narrows or changes a plan, a yes to an offer that no longer stands should not be recorded. It showed up on two scenarios, three models, and it is the one thing the held-out set caught.
  • The remaining misses are specific. A weekend day named on that weekend's morning; a yes to a time that has already passed; "make it virtual" with no field to put it in; a Q1 ask in November. Each is a filed build request, and each is listed in layer 2.

Read these before quoting a number

  • Bob built the harness and wrote the expectations. The design and its predictions were committed before any run, the grading is code, and two of the four predictions came out wrong. The six held-out scenarios exist to catch the harness measuring fixes to this study's own findings, and they did.
  • The seen set is partly the harness grading its own fixes. Eleven build requests from this study landed in Winnie on 2026-11-09, before the big run. The held-out numbers are the ones without that.
  • Ten runs per cell, and they are not independent. Enough to tell 70% from 90%; not enough to tell 84% from 88%. The held-out differences are inside their error bars. The ten runs of one event share that event, so the error bars, which treat runs as independent, are narrower than the evidence warrants. The unit to reason about is the event: the map in layer 2 and appendix B of the write-up are the per-event views.
  • One person's calendar, scripted replies, one provider. Real cases, narrow cases. Replies from the other party follow a script. Only Claude models ran.
  • The "model alone" condition is a construction. It has the same rules in plain words and tools shaped like a calendar and mail integration, but it is still the bench's idea of a bare deployment.

Layer 2 · How it was measured

Each finding, taken apart

The words used throughout, then every finding with the table behind it and what to look at. Each number here can be re-derived from the run files with one command.

The words

Scenario. One frozen meeting to schedule: a fixed "now", Bob's calendar, the people, the email thread so far, and what happens next. 32 scenarios the harness was built against (the seen set) and 6 written afterwards in shapes no build request named (the held-out set).

Event. One thing happening on a scenario: a reply arrives, Bob sends a Telegram line, a timer fires. The model acts once per event. 48 seen events, 8 held-out.

Seed. One repetition. Models are not deterministic, so every event ran ten times per condition per model. A cell of the big table is 480 runs: 48 events × 10 seeds.

Condition. A = the model alone with raw tools. B = Winnie's harness with its guards computing their refusals but not enforcing them. C = the full harness. Same model, same scenario, three ways.

Outcome match. Did the run end where Bob said it should? Each event has an expected decision (offer times, ask the other person, ask Bob, book it, wait, close), the people it must reach, and the times it may use. Code checks all of that. Bob read and confirmed every expectation.

Violation. A rule broken on the way, whatever the outcome. Nine rules, graded by code using the harness's own checks: used a time nobody offered or checked (V1), booked before everyone said yes (V2), offered a time outside the hours asked (V3), converted a zone wrong (V4), wrote to someone not on the thread (V5), made a decision that was Bob's (V6), ignored something Bob said earlier (V7), kept acting after the meeting was closed (V8), ran out of steps (V9).

Prose rule. Four checks on the wording of what the model wrote to Bob and to the other people, read by a judge model and checked against Bob's own labels on 30 texts.

A worked example

Scenario h03, hours narrowed. Bob asked for 45 minutes with Felix the week of December 7. Winnie offered a Monday afternoon, a Tuesday morning and a Thursday afternoon. Event 0: Bob writes on Telegram, "Mornings only for this one please." Expected: offer mornings again. Event 1: Felix writes, "Thursday at 3 works for me." Expected: do not take that yes; offer the mornings again, or tell him the afternoon is withdrawn, or bring it to Bob. A run that records Felix's yes on the withdrawn Thursday breaks rule V7 (ignored something Bob said earlier), even if nothing gets booked.

What to look at. The A→B column is the scaffolding: a rendered picture of the plan, slot ids that cannot be invented, tools that answer truthfully. The B→C column is the guards actually refusing. The guards' share grows with the model: Sonnet 5.5 gets a quarter of its lift from enforcement, Haiku a ninth.

Where the model alone fails. It books Thanksgiving when the calendar shows the day free. It books "let's do 7" at the wrong meal or refuses to read it. It books over a third person's times without asking Bob. It cannot search inside a party's windows. On both Sonnets it breaks a constraint set in turn 1 by turn 6.

Where the harness still fails. A small set of events, each a filed build request: a weekend day named on that weekend's morning (nobody gets it); a yes to a time that has already passed (Sonnet 5 gets it, Haiku and Sonnet 5.5 do not); Bob's "make it 30 minutes and virtual" with no field for "virtual"; a Q1 ask in November. Sonnet 5.5 under the full harness misses 23 of 480, and 20 of them are the first two.

Why this set exists. On 2026-11-09 Winnie took eleven build requests that this study had filed and shipped them as one change. The seen set then measures, in part, fixes to its own findings. What it says. The current models handle five of the six new shapes as well on their own as under the harness. The sixth, h03, is where the harness itself goes wrong: Bob says mornings only, the other person says yes to a withdrawn afternoon, and the harness records that yes on every model (4 and 6 of 10 on Haiku, 4 and 7 on Sonnet 5, 8 and 7 on Sonnet 5.5, in B and C). Six scenarios and 80 runs a cell is a small set, and the error bars say so. The direction is the finding, not the size.

Finding 6 and 8 · The wording, and whether a model can judge it

A judge model (Sonnet 5) read every text the models wrote to Bob and to the other people, 6,620 of them, against the facts of its run, for four slips: P1 said it was done when it was only drafted; P2 promised to keep watching when nothing was set up; P3 gave a reason the facts do not support; P4 got a time, day or person wrong.

First, is the judge any good? Before any verdict existed, 30 texts were set aside for Bob to label by hand on a page that hid the condition and the model. The first judge pass failed three of its four rules against his labels. Every false alarm was a fact the judge had not been shown (Bob is in cc of every harness mail; Bob's own lines; the plan's mode; when earlier mails went out) or a "next step if they reply" that the second rule's wording did not settle. The facts were widened, that rule was reworded, and the whole pass ran again. Against the same 30 labels:

P1 is the clean result. Alone, the model tells the other person the call "is confirmed" while the mail is still held for Bob, in 10 to 14% of runs. The harness frames every mail as a draft behind a card, and that takes it to 0 to 3% on every model and on the held-out set too. Bob's one disagreement with the judge:

Sonnet 5.5, alone, to Bob · Bob says this is P1, the judge does notQuinn agreed to Monday, Nov 16, 12:15–12:45 PM for the 30-minute video call. I've put the event on your calendar with Quinn invited, and it's waiting for your approval.

P2 is rare everywhere once "I'll book it when they confirm" stops counting. Neither Bob nor the judge found one in the sample. No harness effect is claimed.

P3 points the harness's way but softly. The harness is below bare on every model and both sets, by about half. But the judge still fires on things Bob does not count, two extra in 30:

Sonnet 5.5, harness, to a party · judge says P3, Bob says noTuesday at 1:15 pm does not work on Bob's side, so here are some times next week.

P4 is the judge's failure. With its facts fixed it fires almost never, and it missed all three slips Bob caught:

Sonnet 5, alone, to Bob · Bob says P4 (the slots were sixty minutes), the judge missed itGot it—updated the meeting to 30 minutes, virtual. I've sent Quinn the same three time options (shortened to 30 min) and it's waiting in your approval queue.

So the P4 row is a floor, not a rate. In the sample a human found a wrong detail in 3 of 26 texts; the judge found none. The study reports that sentence instead of the row.

The method lesson. The judge's false alarms were not in its rubric but in its facts. Reading thirty texts against a human's labels found that; the agreement numbers alone would not have.

What did not run

Opus 5 and Opus 5.5. Every Opus 5.5 step, 1,680 of 1,680, came back refused by the API in under a second with zero output, category "reasoning extraction". Probes found the trigger: Winnie's step format asks for a short "thought" beside each tool call, and that sentence alone is refused 3 of 3; a neutral schema of the same shape passes, and Sonnet 5.5 answers the same prompt. The bench has no fallback model by design, since a fallback changes the model under test. Bob's ruling: report Opus as not run, cause stated; a re-run under a changed format is deferred. Winnie's own prompt is the one the harness sends, so the same would meet her on an Opus-class model (build request 19). Cost $109.

GPT and Gemini. Adapters exist; no keys were supplied. Bob's ruling: Anthropic models only on this run.

What goes back to Winnie

Nineteen build requests were filed during the study, one withdrawn. Eleven landed in Winnie on 2026-11-09 and the big run measured the harness with them in. What the run adds, on the full harness:

  • Request 3, drop a slot whose time has passed: holds on Sonnet 5 (8 of 10) and not on Haiku (1) or Sonnet 5.5 (0, where five runs book the past slot).
  • Request 16, "make it virtual" has no field: Haiku 0, Sonnet 5 2, Sonnet 5.5 10 of 10. Haiku asks Bob what he just said.
  • Request 17, the prompt and the yes-guard disagree on a window slot: Sonnet 5 only, the guard refusing ten attempts.
  • Request 9, a weekend day named on that weekend's morning: 0 of 10 in every cell but Sonnet 5 B 6 and C 4. Every model guesses.
  • Request 6, a Q1 ask in November: Sonnet 5 C 2 of 10; Haiku and Sonnet 5.5 ask the party 10 of 10.
  • The stale yes (h03, and s10 event 1): the one new finding from the held-out set, filed after Bob reads it.

Model misses proper under the full harness, after those are set aside: on Sonnet 5.5, three runs of 480.

Limitations, in full

  • Bob built the harness and wrote the expectations. The mitigations: pre-registered predictions, two of which failed; code graders using the harness's own checks; every re-ruling dated with its moved lines counted; a held-out set.
  • The "model alone" condition is a construction: the same rules in a human assistant's words, tools shaped like a calendar and mail integration, no delete, no colleague calendar beyond free/busy. Its raw calls are mapped to the harness's vocabulary by fixed rules, two of which Bob decided after the runs.
  • One person's calendar and one assistant's cases; synthetic scenarios are the study's idea of what goes wrong; one weekly calendar pattern laid over each scenario's dates.
  • The other party's replies follow a script. Nobody is rude, wrong or evasive beyond the script.
  • Ten seeds: about four points of interval on the seen set, ten to fifteen on the held-out set. The seeds of one event are not independent trials; the intervals are over lines and so are generous, and the event is the unit to reason about.
  • One provider, two tiers. Opus did not run. The prompt was tuned on Claude models.
  • The prose judge is a model, checked against one human labeller, who built the harness. P4 failed that check and is not a rate.
  • The harness changed twice during the study as Winnie took the study's own requests; the violation checks are frozen at the earlier version so they mean the same thing in every column. Forty numbered rulings record every change.
